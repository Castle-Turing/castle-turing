# Reading is commissioned, never curated

**What.** Stated as direction by the resident, 2026-09-06. The night's
work produced five research reports and their rendered artifacts —
but every one existed because a session was asked, in the moment, to
go find out. Nothing keeps reading material *ready*. The gap has two
faces, and the resident named both:

1. **Workflow.** Grounding work in published prior art should be
   standing practice, not an ad-hoc virtue: design work commissions or
   cites its literature before committing, and the library
   (`docs/research/`, with its trust discipline and rendered
   artifacts) is the accumulating result. Tonight is the precedent;
   nothing yet makes it the norm.
2. **The Primer role.** Castle Turing's premise — pulled up and
   restated by the resident against the minutiae of the night — is
   that the ultimate goal of AI systems is to elevate humanity. Part
   of that, concretely: based on context, the resident model, and
   potentially a cognitive model of the resident, synthesize and
   suggest appropriate reading, the way a great assistant or chief of
   staff would. Not search results — curation: this, now, because of
   what you are in the middle of and where you want to grow.

**Why it matters.** The vision's comprehension principle says the
resident is owed a current map and, in domains they want to grow in,
doing over being shown — and the library already holds the research
behind that (`competence-as-an-end.md` on building the resident, not
just the system; `staying-fluent.md` on staying a capable supervisor
of work beyond one's expertise). Curated reading is the lightest
instrument that serves both: it costs the resident only attention
they choose to spend, and it feeds exactly the demonstrated-competence
signal the authority taxonomy wants to run on.

**Existing pieces this composes with, rather than duplicates.**
The resident model exists (and
`workers-do-not-read-the-resident-model.md` records that workers
ignore it); `apprenticeship-has-no-mechanism.md` and
`competence-measurement-entrustment.md` hold the growth-and-trust
side; the digest and handover are the natural delivery channels. A
"cognitive model of the resident" beyond the current resident model
would be new mechanism — the resident's own words flagged it as
*potentially*, and it should be treated as an open design question,
not an assumption.

**Constraints already binding on any design.** Proposal 05's grammar:
a suggestion is an offer, never an assignment, and only the resident
closes it. Proposal 06: whether the resident read something is a
receipt — it may inform which offers come next, it may never grade
the resident or silently retune what is offered without the question
channel. And the trust discipline of the library applies to curation
too: a suggested reading arrives with why-this-why-now and what it
claims, not as a bare link.

**Open questions.** Whether the workflow face is a convention line
(briefs cite prior art or state that none was sought) or something
with a checker behind it, per the prescriptive-formats direction;
where curation runs (a seat with journal access reading the current
milestone and recent work, plausibly) and at what cadence; and
whether this belongs in the vision — the resident has said a vision
addition on self-assembly and elevation may be coming, and that
writing is the resident's, not a seat's.
