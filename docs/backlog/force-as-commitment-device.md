# Will the resident keep a system that enforces their better judgment?

*A research question, not deferred implementation. Citations from
memory; **[verify]** marks the unconfirmed.*

**What.** `docs/vision.md`: "Force is the escalation of a conversation,
not the opening move" — ambient cue, then nudge, then "the hard
workspace swap reserved for genuinely hard commitments." Its warrant is
"serve stated preferences, not just revealed ones": the system enforces
who the resident said they want to be against who they are at 2pm on
Hacker News. Morning focus mode is the project's starting slice, so
this ships early.

*Boundary: the completed interruption research covers when and how to
interrupt. This is about the legitimacy and durability of coercion —
whether enforcement retains consent — not its timing.*

**The question.** Do externally-enforced commitment mechanisms sustain
consent and effect over months, or do reactance and attrition lead to
circumvention and disabling? And does effectiveness depend on the
constraint being self-imposed per-commitment rather than held as
standing authority?

The vision already observes that an assistant which interrupts badly
gets disabled within a week. The same dynamic presumably applies to one
that coerces badly — and the literature has measured it.

**Where the answer likely lives.** Economics of self-control: Schelling,
"Egonomics, or the Art of Self-Management" (1978); Thaler & Shefrin's
planner-doer model (1981); Bryan, Karlan & Nelson's review of commitment
devices (2010) **[verify]** — from memory, take-up is low and
circumvention common, with self-imposed, stakes-bearing devices doing
best; Ariely & Wertenbroch on self-imposed deadlines (2002). HCI: Lyngs
et al., "Self-Control in Cyberspace" (CHI 2019) **[verify]** on digital
self-control tools and their attrition. Psychology: Brehm's reactance
theory (1966); self-determination theory on controlling versus
autonomy-supportive interventions — directly relevant to whether the
*graduated* ladder, which preserves choice until the last rung, is the
shape SDT predicts survives.

Also worth reading against the vision's own escape valve ("sometimes the
rabbit hole is where the good idea comes from"): the incubation and
mind-wandering literature **[verify usefulness]**.

**What would change.** The force tier might require per-commitment
opt-in — a Ulysses contract named in the stated-priorities document,
with an expiry — rather than standing authority. Overrides of force
would be recorded as verdicts, which Proposal 06's machinery already
fits. The ladder might gain a consent-renewal rung. If the literature
says standing enforcement simply gets disabled, the honest design
response is to make disabling *legible* — a journaled verdict — rather
than pretending it will not happen.

**Priority: medium.** Focus mode is in the founding slice, but the
coercive rungs are explicitly deferred until trust accrues. There is
time to read first, and the reading likely restructures the ladder.
