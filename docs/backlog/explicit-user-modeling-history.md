# Why did user modeling stop asking?

*A research question, not deferred implementation. The most purely
educational entry here — the resident would come to understand why the
entire industry went the way the project defines itself against.*

**What.** The resident model is explicit, inspectable, one entry per
fact, written only through elicited or volunteered resident statements
(Proposal 05: "Inference may open a question; only the resident may
close it"). Set that against a historical fact: an entire research field
built explicit user models for two decades, then largely abandoned them
for implicit behavioural ones.

*Boundary: the completed corrections research covers stated-versus-
revealed preference as a signal question. This is the systems-history
question — why explicit modelling lost, and whether its losing
conditions still hold.*

**The question.** Were the reasons explicit user modelling lost —
elicitation burden, users declining to maintain profiles, preference
instability, model staleness — fundamental, or artifacts of
pre-conversational interfaces whose costs a natural-language agent
removes? Proposal 05's cost paragraph ("the system stays wrong longer
than a behavioural learner would") is a bet that the burden term has
changed. The field's history is the record of what happens if it hasn't.

**Where the answer likely lives.** The user-modelling tradition: Rich's
GRUNDY and stereotypes (1979); the UMUAI journal; Kobsa on generic
user-modelling systems **[verify]**. Most directly, **Judy Kay's
scrutable user models and the Personis systems** — user-inspectable,
user-controllable models, which is this exact artifact, with published
findings on whether and when subjects actually inspect and correct them
**[verify system names]**. The resident model appears to be a
rediscovery of that programme, and reading it is probably the single
highest-value item in this entry. The 1997 Shneiderman–Maes debate
(direct manipulation versus interface agents) for the control question.

**A finding already in hand that bears on this.** Lichtenstein & Slovic
(eds.), *The Construction of Preference* (2006): people do not hold
complete preference orderings waiting to be read out — they construct
them at elicitation, and normatively equivalent procedures produce
systematically different answers. If so, **a model entry is a fact about
a conversation, not a durable fact about a person**, and needs
re-confirmation semantics. Task 0010 already captures the intake surface
on correction records for exactly this reason.

**What would change.** Model entries gain context and staleness
semantics — a dated, situated statement with a re-confirmation cadence
rather than a timeless fact. The apprenticeship design borrows whatever
the scrutability programme learned about getting residents to actually
look at their model. If the history instead shows elicitation failed
only on interface cost, Proposal 05 gets a strong vindication story to
cite when it is promoted to a principle.

**Priority: lower.** Proposal 05 is implemented and its hardening test
is well-formed; this refines semantics rather than blocking work.
